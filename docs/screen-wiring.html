WALL·E build — step 3a

Wiring the screen to the board

Eight wires go from the back of the little screen to numbered pins on the Radxa board. Nothing is soldered. Nothing is cut.

8 wires ~20 minutes female–to–female power OFF throughout

Before you touch anything

Unplug the board first.

No USB cable, no battery, no power bank. Wires pushed onto a live board can kill the screen in a second. Plug power in only at the very end.

The red wire goes to pin 1, never pin 2 or pin 4.

Pin 1 gives 3.3 volts, which is what the screen wants. Pins 2 and 4 give 5 volts and will burn it out. They are right next to each other, so count carefully. On the drawing below those two pins are ringed in red.

What you need

The screen module, the Radxa board, and eight female-to-female jumper wires — the kind with a little plastic socket on both ends. Both the screen and the board have bare metal pins sticking up, so you need sockets at each end. Wires with a pin on one end will not fit.

Finding the right pin on the board

The board has forty pins in two long rows. They are not numbered 1–20 down one side and 21–40 down the other. They go in pairs across:

1 and 2 are side by side. Below them, 3 and 4. Then 5 and 6. And so on.

So the odd numbers run down one row and the even numbers run down the other.

Pin 1 is the corner where the solder pad underneath is a square instead of a circle — every other pad is round. Turn the board over and look; it is the only square one. That corner is pin 1, and pin 2 is its neighbour.

To find pin 17, count down the odd row from pin 1: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 — the ninth pin down. Count it twice.

Do it in this order

  1. Unplug the board. Everything out. Check the power bank is disconnected too.
  2. Turn the screen over. The glass faces down. Along one edge you will see a row of nine metal pins with names printed next to them on the board.
  3. Find pin 1 on the Radxa board. The square solder pad. Put a small dot of marker pen or a sticker next to it so you never lose it.
  4. Do the red wire first: VCC to pin 1. Push each socket straight down onto the pin. Press until it stops. Do not rock it side to side — the pins bend.
  5. Then the black wire: GND to pin 6. Pin 6 is in the even row, the third one down: 2, 4, 6.
  6. Then the other six, one at a time. Work down the list above and tick each one off. Finish a wire completely before picking up the next one. This is where mistakes happen.
  7. Leave SDO empty. Nine pins, eight wires. One stays bare. That is correct.
  8. Count everything again. Go back to pin 1 and count along the board for each wire. Two minutes now saves a dead screen.
  9. Now plug the power in. Watch the screen. See the next section for what should happen.

Check before you switch on

  • Nothing is plugged into the red wire except pin 1.
  • No wire is on pin 2 or pin 4.
  • Every socket is pushed fully down — no metal pin still showing.
  • No two wires share a pin.
  • Exactly one screen pin (SDO) is empty.
  • No loose wire is touching the metal of the board.

What should happen — and what should not

Expected: the screen lights up white or grey, and stays that way.

That is a pass. The backlight is working and the screen has power. It will stay blank until the software is set up — the board has not been told to draw anything yet. A blank lit screen is a working screen at this stage.

Pull the power out immediately if…

anything feels warm, you smell anything at all, or the board does not start. Unplug, then check the red wire and the black wire before anything else. Those two are the only wires that can do damage.

A screen that stays completely dark usually means the LED wire is on the wrong pin, or is not pushed all the way down. That one is harmless — just power off and re-seat it.
