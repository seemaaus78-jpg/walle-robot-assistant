WALL·E build — master sheet

Every wire

Thirty of the forty pins, in one place: screen, touch, audio, tracks, servos, camera and the way back in when it all goes quiet. Wired in the order below so a mistake shows up while there is still room to reach it.

How power flows

Motor power never comes from the board.

Two N20s stall at well over an amp between them. Pulled through the Radxa's 5 V pin that is a brownout mid-sentence, and a corrupted SD card if it happens during a write. Battery to driver, board to driver for logic only.

And the grounds must be joined.

The driver reads your GPIO as high or low relative to its own ground. With separate grounds it sees noise and the motors twitch at random. One wire from a board GND pin to the driver's GND, always.

The 3.3 V problem

The A7Z brings 3.3 V out on pins 1 and 17 only. Three things want it: the screen's logic, the screen's backlight, and the microphone. One pin has to feed two of them.

Put the two small loads together.

Pin 1 feeds the screen's VCC and the mic — about 12 mA between them. Pin 17 feeds the backlight alone, which is the hungry one at 50 mA or more. Splice with a short length of copper: one wire into pin 1, two out.

The amplifier is the exception — it runs on 5 V from pin 2, which is free, and it needs the higher voltage to get any volume out of a small speaker.

Four pins carry two wires each: 1, 12, 19, 23 — the spliced 3.3 V, the shared I²S clock and word-select, and the two SPI lines the screen and the touch chip both listen on. Only one jumper socket fits on a pin, so those need two wires crimped into one connector or joined just behind it.

Four still free: 3, 5, 32, 36. Pin 32 (PM5) is the spare servo channel, on the same chip as the other four. Pins 27 and 28 look free but are reserved for add-on board identification — leave them.

Servos, and the rule they share with the motors

Four SG90s: neck pan and tilt, one per arm. All four sit on gpiochip1 because the pulses are generated in software from a single line request — split across two chips they would need keeping in step, and a servo that misses a frame twitches.

Banks L and M share that chip at 32 lines each, so the offset is NUM for PL and NUM + 32 for PM. The screen's DC line is PL5 on the same chip — a different line, no clash.

Servo power comes from the battery, exactly like the motors.

An SG90 stalls near 650 mA. Four of them reaching a pose together is over two amps — several times what the board's 5 V pin will pass. Feed them from the shield and run one ground wire back to pin 34, or the board and the servos disagree about what zero volts means and the arms jitter.

Only the thin signal wire goes to the header. Red and brown stay with the battery.

Camera and switch

The Pi Camera you have will not fit this board. The A7Z's connector is 31-pin at 0.3 mm pitch; the camera's ribbon is 15-pin at 1.0 mm, and the Radxa forum has no working report of bridging them. Use a USB webcam into the USB-C 3.1 port with a C-to-A adapter, which leaves the USB-C 2.0 port free for power. Nothing touches the header.

The switch goes inline on the wire from the shield to the board, not on the header and not between the cells and the shield — cutting power upstream of the charger stops it charging too.

Switch the robot off, not the board's power alone.

Put the motors and servos on the switched side as well. Otherwise the tracks stay live with the brain off, which is exactly the state in which a stray command left in the driver drives WALL·E off the table.

Wire it in this order

  1. Power, and stop. Shield to the board over USB-C. Boot it, SSH in, confirm it runs on battery before anything else is attached.
  2. Serial console — pins 8, 9, 10. Three wires, and your way back in when a later step breaks the network.
  3. Screen and touch — twelve wires. Power pair first (1 and 6), then the six signals, then the four touch lines. Test after the screen and before the touch.
  4. Audio — mic, amp, speaker. Both sit on the same clock and word-select. Mic on 3.3 V spliced from pin 1, amp on 5 V from pin 2.
  5. Motors, and on a stand. Battery to driver, four GPIO to driver, ground to pin 30. Prop the robot up so the tracks spin free the first time — if a direction is reversed you want to find out with it off the floor.
  6. Servos one at a time. Signal to its pin, power from the shield, ground to pin 34. Centre each one before the horn goes on, or it will reach its stop and sit there straining. Four at once and you cannot tell which is buzzing.
  7. Camera and switch. Webcam into the USB-C 3.1 port. Switch inline on the shield's output, with the motors and servos on the switched side.
If a motor runs the wrong way, change the config, not the wire.

The two motors face opposite directions, so one is reversed by design. Swap left_forward_line and left_backward_line in config.toml. Five seconds, versus desoldering a motor you have just glued in.
