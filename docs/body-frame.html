WALL·E build — the frame

Blueprint

Sized to the parts on your table, not to a scale factor. The battery decides almost everything: at 100 × 50 mm it is the largest object in the robot, and the body is essentially a box built around it.

Battery 100 plus clearance gives an interior of 107 × 57 mm. Add the brass and the body lands at 110 × 60 mm — which is 1.75× the original sketch lengthwise and 1.88× in depth.

The proportions survive the scaling.

Every edge lands within 1 % of the original's ratios except the depth, which is 7 % chunkier to swallow the battery. It still reads as WALL·E, just slightly broader in the shoulders.

Side profile, with everything in it

The track is too short

60 mm of track on a 110 mm body covers 55 % of it.

WALL·E's tracks run nearly the whole length of his body. Yours would stop just past halfway and leave 50 mm of bare brass at the back, which will read as wrong even to someone who has never looked at the film closely.

Three ways out, in the order I would try them:

OptionCostResult
Add chain links and a third idler sprocket a few more links Best. Chain toy track is sold by the link; extending to ~100 mm keeps everything else as drawn
One 18650 instead of two half the runtime Battery drops to ~65 mm, body to ~75 mm, and the 60 mm track fits properly
Move the battery into a rear hump changes the silhouette Body shortens over the tracks, but it stops looking like WALL·E

Measure your chain first: if it is sold in links you can almost certainly buy or cannibalise more, and everything on this page stays as drawn.

Cut list

All 1.5 mm brass. Bend one side, then match the second to the first rather than to the paper — two sides that agree with each other sit square.

110mm bottom ×2
62mm front slope ×2
37mm top ×2
54mm rear slope ×2
40mm back ×2
60mm cross pieces ×8

1.09 m of brass in total. Cut 1.4 m — the first bend of anything is the one you get wrong, and the rear slope is the fiddly one.

Fit the battery before the last two cross pieces.

It clears the walls by three millimetres on each side. Drop it in while the frame is still open at the top, and if it fouls anywhere you can still spread the sides. After the top cross pieces go on, you cannot.
