WALL·E build — the frame

Bending the body

There are no cut lengths in the original. You print a sheet, lay the brass on the printed line, bend it to match, and cut where the line stops. But that sheet is drawn around parts far smaller than yours.

What the original sheet says

The creator's Structural Sketch is one A4 page at exactly 300 DPI, so printing it at 100 % — not “fit to page” — gives a true 1:1 template. Five numbers are marked on it:

6.3cm — bottom edge
3.5cm — front slope
2.1cm — top edge
2.3cm — back edge
3.2cm ×8 — cross pieces

Two identical side profiles, joined by eight cross pieces. The cross pieces are what set the body's width, so the finished box is 6.3 × 3.5 × 3.2 cm.

Why you cannot use it as printed

That body was built around a XIAO ESP32S3 — a board about the size of a postage stamp — and a 1.54″ screen. You are building the same robot around a Radxa and a 2.8″ panel. Measure them against a 6.3 cm box:

PartSizeInto a 6.3 × 3.5 × 3.2 cm body?
Radxa Cubie A7Z6.5 × 3.0 cm No — longer than the body itself
18650 cell + shield~7.2 × 2.6 cm No — also longer than the body
2.8″ display module5.0 × 8.6 cm No — the head would dwarf the body
Speaker, amp, mic, driversmall Yes — but nowhere left to put them
The board alone is longer than the body.

Not close, not tight — 6.5 cm of board into 6.3 cm of box. Print that sheet at 100 %, cut brass to it, and none of your electronics goes inside. This is the one thing to get right before any metal is cut.

Double everything

Scale the whole frame to 200 %. Every number doubles, the proportions stay exactly WALL·E, and the box becomes 12.6 × 7.0 × 6.4 cm — which swallows the board, the battery and the wiring with room to work in.

Cut list

126mm bottom×2
70mm front slope×2
42mm top×2
46mm back×2
62mm rear slope×2
64mm cross pieces×8

Roughly 1.2 m of brass in total. Buy 1.5 m — the first bend of anything is always the one you get wrong.

Two thicknesses, two jobs.

1.5 mm brass is the skeleton: it holds shape and takes the weight. 1 mm copper is soft enough to thread through a component's mounting hole, which is how the boards attach without brackets.

Order of work

  1. Draw the profile full size on paper. Double every number off the original sheet and rule it out, or print the sheet at 200 %. Either way, measure the drawn line with a ruler before cutting anything — printers lie about scale.
  2. Bend one side, then match the second to it. Not to the drawing — to the first one. Two sides that agree with each other sit square; two that each agree with the paper do not.
  3. Solder the eight cross pieces. Corners first, then the middles. The frame is floppy until roughly the fifth joint, then suddenly rigid.
  4. Test fit the board and battery before closing it up. Drop them in loose. Anything that does not fit now will not fit once there are wires in the way.
  5. Head, tracks and arms last. The head carries the 2.8″ panel, so build it around the module you actually have rather than around the drawing.
